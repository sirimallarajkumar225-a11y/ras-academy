Hardware Error Evidence Correlation

Build a disciplined investigation from multiple records without jumping to conclusions or treating one log as a complete diagnosis.

Why correlation matters

Server hardware events may be surfaced through firmware, platform management controllers, operating-system logs, subsystem tools and application observations. These sources may have different scopes, retention policies, clocks, identifiers and detail. Agreement can strengthen an interpretation; differences can be evidence gaps rather than proof of a fault.

A practical correlation workflow

  1. Define the question: record the symptom, timeframe, scope and impact.
  2. Preserve records: retain source messages and collection context; redact sensitive information.
  3. Normalize time: document timezone, clock offsets and uncertainties.
  4. Map sources: distinguish OS, firmware, management and workload evidence.
  5. Compare: look for corroboration, recurrence and contextual changes.
  6. Evaluate: clearly label confirmed facts, interpretations and unresolved questions.
  7. Plan: choose proportionate next steps using approved procedures.

Evidence versus hypothesis

Observation: a record was reported by a particular source during a documented interval.

Hypothesis: the record may indicate a subsystem issue. A hypothesis requires additional support and should not be written as a confirmed root cause.

Record contradictory evidence and uncertainty. Avoid attributing a failure to a specific component solely from a counter, error category or isolated log line.
